Incidence — a quantity, and no law at all

      

        A ray arrives at a surface at some angle. That is the whole chapter: one quantity, its full
        name, the short name the equations will use, and the branch it lives on. There is no
        equation here, so there is nothing to solve — and that turns out to be the
        clearest way to see what the rest of the engine is doing.

        Drag the dividers to resize the panes; double-click one to put it back. Tick
        auto-run and the laws re-evaluate when you stop typing, and the page boots Ruby
        by itself next time. Both settings are remembered between visits.
      

      

        Anything you change is marked the way an editor marks it: a bar in the gutter beside every
        line you touched — green for new, blue for altered — a red wedge where lines were taken
        out, a dot on the tab and an M in the tree. Revert edits puts
        this repository's own text back.
      

      

        Declaring is not solving. surface[:i] hands back the number you gave it, and
        so does surface.solve(:i) — an answer already known is not solved twice. Ask a
        scenario that was given nothing, though, and it refuses: nothing here determines an angle
        of incidence. It is what you bring to the problem, not what the problem tells you. Every
        later chapter adds an equation, and from then on there is always a law in the way of seeing
        this.
      

      

        alias: :i is why sin(i) can appear in Snell's law two chapters
        from now and still mean this. Both names lead to the same variable, so a value given as
        angle_of_incidence: is readable as [:i] and the other way round.
      

      

        within: is the branch the quantity physically lives on. It does nothing yet —
        with no equation there is no root to find — but it travels with the quantity into every law
        that includes this one, and in chapter 1.3 it is the difference between an answer and a
        root several turns away.
      

      

        Every other law in this book begins include Incidence. That is the whole of
        its job: say once what an arriving ray is, so that three laws can talk about the same one.
