notes

      

        The two outgoing rays share the incident ray's width exactly, so four percent looks like
        four percent. Two well-known facts fall out of these equations rather than being stated:
        reflection reaches 1 at the critical angle, which is what the total in total
        internal reflection means, and the p-polarised share reaches 0 at Brewster's angle —
        56.31° for glass — which is why polarised sunglasses cut glare off water.
      

      

        Approaching the critical angle the collapse is steeper than it looks. Set μ₁ 1.50 and μ₂
        1.00 and creep from 41.00° upward: 62% still refracts at 41.00°, 2% at 41.81°, and nothing
        at all a hundredth of a degree later. cos(rr) is going to zero as the
        refracted ray swings flat along the surface, and it takes the whole transition with it.
