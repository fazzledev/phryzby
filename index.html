phryzby — play with physics, learn with ruby

Why do kids love to play sports, but not to study?

Nobody learns football by reading about football. You get the ball, you try something, and the ball answers straight away. Nothing is at stake, so you try the daft thing too, and the daft thing is where most of the learning happens.

Studying physics goes the other way round. The law arrives finished, in a textbook, and the only move left to you is to agree with it. There is nothing to try, and nothing that answers back.

So here the law is the ball.

the whole of lib/light/refraction.rb

require_relative "incidence"

module Refraction
  extend Physics::Law

  include Incidence

  quantity :angle_of_refraction,       variable: :rr, within: Physics::A_RIGHT_ANGLE
  quantity :relative_refractive_index, variable: :mu21

  equation(:snells_law) { mu21 == sin(i) / sin(rr) }

  condition(:no_refracted_ray) { sin(i) / mu21 > 1 }
end

and every question it answers

glass = Physics::Scenario.including(Refraction)

glass.new(i: 30.deg, mu21: 1.5)
     .solve(:rr).in_degrees
=> 19.4712     # the refracted ray

glass.new(i: 30.deg, rr: 19.4712.deg)
     .solve(:mu21)
=> 1.5000      # the pair of media

glass.new(rr: 90.deg, mu21: 1.0 / 1.5)
     .solve(:i).in_degrees
=> 41.8103     # the critical angle

Every chapter below runs CRuby compiled to WebAssembly and evaluates this repository's own .rb files, the same bytes the tests run, with an irb underneath it against the same VM.

The machinery

  • 0 The Engine What a law is made of: an expression tree where == builds instead of compares, the scope a declaration runs in, and a solver that does not care which side of the equals sign the unknown was on.

Light

  • 1.1 Incidence One quantity and no law at all — a full name, the short name the equations will use, and the branch it lives on. Declaring is not solving: ask a scenario that was given nothing and it refuses. Every later law begins include Incidence.
  • 1.2 Reflection The first equation, and what it means for == to build rather than compare. A law is a module and holds nothing; Physics::Scenario.including(Reflection) is what holds values and solves.
  • 1.3 Refraction: Air to Water Air into water, and one slider. A second law composed alongside the first, neither a kind of the other, and the first equation the engine cannot rearrange: it finds the root. Declared domains, because a periodic law has infinitely many and only one is physical.
  • 1.4 Refraction: Water to Air The same crossing the other way, bending away from the normal instead. Slide far enough round and the refracted ray lies flat along the surface and then is not there at all — a declared condition, not an error.
  • 1.5 Internal Reflection The crossing again, with the ray that does not cross. Two laws composed, each answering for its own ray out of the one scenario, and past the angle where the refracted one goes the reflected one is all that is left.
  • 1.6 Critical Angle The angle the last chapter found by sliding, named and worked out. No law is added and no formula: it is Snell asked backwards, with the refracted ray lying flat along the surface, which is the last angle that still has one.
  • 1.7 Refractive Index That the pair reduces to one number each is a claim of its own, and it is one file: μ₂₁ = μ₂ / μ₁. Set both and watch it against sin i ÷ sin rᵣ, which is Snell found rather than stated.
  • 1.8 Any Two Media A second law composed alongside the first, neither a kind of the other. Declared domains, because a periodic law has infinitely many roots and only one is physical. Media are named here and not numbered: what bends the ray is the two of them together.
  • 1.9 Reflectance Fresnel: how much goes each way. Three more equations, and one question — solve(:r) — that reaches back through Snell for an angle nobody asked for. Then, past the critical angle, it runs out of answers.
  • 1.10 Total Internal Reflection A law that only sometimes holds. One equation, r == 1, guarded by when: — so the solver reaches for it only where Snell has no solution, and the word total stops being a claim in prose.

No gems, no build step, no framework. 139 tests, Ruby 3.4.
