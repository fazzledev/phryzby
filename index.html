phryzby play with physics, learn with ruby

Why do kids love their playground, but not the classroom?

In the playground they are free — throwing and kicking, running and jumping, shouting and laughing, touching and feeling. With sticks and stones, swings and slides, sand and water, they can do anything. And whatever they try, the world responds.

In the classroom they are held still — sit, don't move; listen, don't talk; do this, don't do that. They can read and write about Newton's laws, but not yet be a scientist. To try, fail, and actually learn, they must wait a decade or two.

phryzby is a playground to break laws, try ruby, and build physics.

the whole of lib/flight/projectile.rb

require_relative "../physics"

# Throw something and let go of it. Nothing pushes it after that, so where it
# goes is settled the instant it leaves your hand — by how fast it left, the
# angle it left at, and how hard the world pulls down.
module Projectile
  extend Physics::Law

  quantity :speed,          variable: :u
  quantity :angle_of_throw, variable: :theta, within: Physics::A_RIGHT_ANGLE
  quantity :gravity,        variable: :g

  quantity :range,          variable: :x
  quantity :peak,           variable: :h
  quantity :time_of_flight, variable: :t

  equation(:how_far)  { x == u**2 * sin(2 * theta) / g }
  equation(:how_high) { h == (u * sin(theta))**2 / (2 * g) }
  equation(:how_long) { t == 2 * u * sin(theta) / g }
end

Every chapter below runs CRuby compiled to WebAssembly and evaluates this repository's own .rb files, the same bytes the tests run, with an irb underneath it against the same VM.

The machinery

  • 0 The Engine What a law is made of: an expression tree where == builds instead of compares, the scope a declaration runs in, and a solver that does not care which side of the equals sign the unknown was on.

Flight

  • 1.1 Projectile Three equations and no optics at all — the same quantity, equation and solve, on a thrown ball. Ask it where the ball lands, or ask it backwards for the angle that reaches a mark, and the engine does not notice the difference.

Light

  • 2.1 Incidence One quantity and no law at all — a full name, the short name the equations will use, and the branch it lives on. Declaring is not solving: ask a scenario that was given nothing and it refuses. Every later law begins include Incidence.
  • 2.2 Reflection The first equation, and what it means for == to build rather than compare. A law is a module and holds nothing; Physics::Scenario.including(Reflection) is what holds values and solves.
  • 2.3 Refraction: Air to Water Air into water, and one slider. A second law composed alongside the first, neither a kind of the other, and the first equation the engine cannot rearrange: it finds the root. Declared domains, because a periodic law has infinitely many and only one is physical.
  • 2.4 Refraction: Water to Air The same crossing the other way, bending away from the normal instead. Slide far enough round and the refracted ray lies flat along the surface and then is not there at all — a declared condition, not an error.
  • 2.5 Internal Reflection The crossing again, with the ray that does not cross. Two laws composed, each answering for its own ray out of the one scenario, and past the angle where the refracted one goes the reflected one is all that is left.
  • 2.6 Critical Angle The angle the last chapter found by sliding, named and worked out. No law is added and no formula: it is Snell asked backwards, with the refracted ray lying flat along the surface, which is the last angle that still has one.
  • 2.7 Into Air The angle just named was water's. Every medium has one of its own, and walking down the list the refracted ray swings flat and goes while the red mark climbs toward the incident one. A chapter of one control and no new law.
  • 2.8 Refractive Index One number each, on two sliders, with the media named along them. No new law: Snell asked for both from the start, and every chapter since 2.3 was holding a pair of them still.
  • 2.9 Relative Refractive Index That both numbers never matter separately is a claim of its own, and it is one file: μ₂₁ = μ₂ ⁄ μ₁. Call the first medium 1 and the second stands for the pair, which is what a single slider was doing all along.
  • 2.10 Any Two Media Both media chosen by name, from the one table every chapter shares, and the number that bends the ray worked out from the pair rather than typed. No new law: every crossing so far has been one row of this, and the rest of the rows are here — including the ones the ray never gets out of.
  • 2.11 Reflectance Fresnel: how much goes each way. Three more equations, and one question — solve(:r) — that reaches back through Snell for an angle nobody asked for. Then, past the critical angle, it runs out of answers.
  • 2.12 Total Internal Reflection A law that only sometimes holds. One equation, r == 1, guarded by when: — so the solver reaches for it only where Snell has no solution, and the word total stops being a claim in prose.

No gems, no build step, no framework. 213 tests, Ruby 3.4.
